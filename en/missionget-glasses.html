So much work waits
until we get back.

An action item on the way somewhere. An idea in the middle of a meeting. Capturing it is only the start. Later, we still have to find it, explain it again and open the right tool. We wanted to shorten that journey.

We used AG05 glasses and the vendor SDK. Our focus was the experience behind them. Glasses capture speech, a phone carries the conversation, and Xiao Mi (小觅), MissionGet’s assistant, connects the request to the same MissionGet workbench.

  1. Glasses · capture
  2. Phone · talk and confirm
  3. Workbench · work and files

Connected.
But not working yet.

The first version could record and transcribe. But finishing a sentence still meant stopping the recording, sending the text and finding the task entry on a phone. The steps had followed us away from the desk.

Why am I still tapping after I finish speaking?

There is more after “connected.”

A control connection does not mean the microphone is ready. Receiving text does not mean a task ran. People need to know what happened and who takes over next.

  1. 01Connect glasses
  2. 02Capture speech
  3. 03Review request
  4. 04Hand off work

Let the conversation
carry on.

A pause ends a short command; after the reply, listening can continue. Meeting recording gets a separate path, with audio and transcripts kept. Actions that change real work still stop for a person to confirm.

  1. 01Say the request
  2. 02Pause to finish
  3. 03Hear the reply
  4. 04Review and confirm

Let the system finish recording and continue the conversation. Keep the business decision with the person.

Fewer taps.
A deliberate moment to decide.

One MissionGet.
One shared piece of work.

A separate task page was quick to make. Connecting it to the actual platform took more decisions about accounts, messages and files. We brought the phone back into the existing product so the conversation could reach the work on the desktop.

This time, the file showed up at the other end.

We checked the handoff with a test document: a request started on the phone, a file was saved in the workbench, and the same result appeared on desktop. The sentence finally had somewhere to return to.

Then we brought it
into the room.

Glasses, a phone, a workbench and a poster, together on the demo table. A rendered idea became something people could see and ask about.

A few fewer taps.
That is what comes next.

This prototype brought voice, phone and workbench closer together. Photo transfer from the physical glasses button was still awaiting acceptance, and foreground voice interaction has conditions. Next is making those boundaries clearer and more dependable.
